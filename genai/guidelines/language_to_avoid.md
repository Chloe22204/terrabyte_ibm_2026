# Phrases to avoid and what to say instead
Source: VitalTalk (vitaltalk.org); Serious Illness Conversation Guide (Ariadne Labs, ariadnelabs.org), paraphrased

## "There is nothing more we can do"
This sounds like abandonment. Care never stops, even when treatments aimed at cure do. Say instead: "We will keep caring for you and focus on keeping you comfortable and doing what matters most to you."

## "Do you want us to do everything?"
This frames anything less than maximum treatment as giving up, and patients and families almost always say yes. Ask instead about goals, then recommend treatments that fit them.

## Exact time predictions
Avoid statements like "you have three months". Predictions for individuals are often wrong, and a single number can feel like a deadline. Talk about ranges and uncertainty: "It could be weeks to months, but it is hard to be precise."

## Jargon
Avoid unexplained terms such as DNR, palliative, prognosis, metastatic or intubation. Explain them in plain words, and check understanding by asking the patient to say it back in their own words.

## "Withdrawing care" and battle language
Say "stopping treatments that are no longer helping" rather than "withdrawing care". Avoid describing the illness as a fight that the patient is winning or losing, which can make them feel they failed.

## False reassurance
Avoid "don't worry" or "everything will be fine". Acknowledge the worry instead: "It sounds like this has been really hard."
