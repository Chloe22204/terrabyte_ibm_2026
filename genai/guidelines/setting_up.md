# Setting up the conversation
Source: SPIKES protocol (Baile et al., The Oncologist 2000, doi:10.1634/theoncologist.5-4-302); VitalTalk (vitaltalk.org); Living Matters, Agency for Integrated Care Singapore (livingmatters.sg)

## Prepare before you go in
Read the notes: current treatments, recent results, previous discussions, any existing advance care plan, Lasting Power of Attorney or Advance Medical Directive. Know what the treating team thinks the realistic options are, so you do not offer what cannot be given.

## Who should be present
Ask the patient who they would like with them. Include the person they would want to make decisions if they could not. A nurse who knows the patient is helpful. If language is a barrier, use a trained interpreter rather than asking a family member to translate.

## The setting
Find a private room, sit down at eye level, silence phones and allow enough time. Let the patient know at the start how long you have. Avoid starting the conversation during a ward round or in a shared bay.

## Ask permission first
Ask whether it is a good time to talk about the future, and how much detail the patient wants. Some patients want full information; others prefer an overview and want the family to hear the details.

## Family-centred decision making in Singapore
In many Singapore families, decisions about serious illness are made together, and relatives sometimes ask that the patient not be told. Respect the family's concern, but ask the patient directly how much they want to know and how they want family involved. The patient's own preferences come first.
