# Recording the conversation and next steps
Source: Living Matters, Agency for Integrated Care Singapore (livingmatters.sg); Mental Capacity Act and Advance Medical Directive Act (Singapore); NICE NG142 (nice.org.uk/guidance/ng142)

## What to record in the care plan
Record what the patient understands about their illness, their goals and what matters most, their fears, any treatments they would or would not want, who they want as decision maker, who was present, and when the conversation will be revisited. Use the patient's own words where possible.

## Advance care planning in Singapore
Living Matters is Singapore's national advance care planning programme. Trained facilitators help patients record their values and preferences, including a Preferred Plan of Care for those with serious illness. Offer a referral to an ACP facilitator if the patient wants to formalise their wishes.

## Legal documents are separate
A Lasting Power of Attorney names someone to make decisions if the patient loses mental capacity. An Advance Medical Directive states that the patient does not want extraordinary life-sustaining treatment if terminally ill and unconscious. Neither replaces the conversation; check whether either exists and record it.

## Share and revisit
Make sure the plan is visible to the whole care team and shared on transfer to another ward, hospital, nursing home or home care. Review it after major changes in health, after admissions, or when the patient asks. Agree a date for the next conversation if this one only opened the topic.
