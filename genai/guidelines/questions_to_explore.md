# Questions to explore
Source: Themes from the Serious Illness Conversation Guide (Ariadne Labs, ariadnelabs.org), paraphrased; VitalTalk (vitaltalk.org)

## Understanding of the illness
Start by learning what the patient already understands. For example: "What have the doctors told you about your illness so far?" or "How do you feel things have been going lately?" Their answer shows where to begin and corrects misunderstandings early.

## Information preferences
Ask how they like to receive information: "Some people want all the details, others prefer the big picture. What would be best for you?" Ask whether they want family to hear this too.

## Goals and what matters most
Explore what is most important if their health gets worse: "If your health were to get worse, what would matter most to you?" Answers often involve being at home, being with family, being comfortable, or reaching a particular event.

## Fears and worries
Ask directly about fears: "What worries you most about the future?" Common worries are pain, being a burden, breathlessness, money, and leaving family behind. Naming them lets the team address them.

## Sources of strength
Ask what helps them cope: faith, family, community or personal values. These can guide support and spiritual care referrals.

## Trade-offs and limits
Ask what they would and would not be willing to go through for the chance of more time, for example a stay in intensive care, a breathing machine or being fed through a tube. This guides decisions about treatment limits.

## Family and decision maker
Ask how much family knows about their wishes and who should speak for them if they cannot. Encourage them to share their wishes with that person.
