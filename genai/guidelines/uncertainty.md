# Talking about uncertainty
Source: VitalTalk (vitaltalk.org); Serious Illness Conversation Guide (Ariadne Labs, ariadnelabs.org), paraphrased

## Talk in ranges
Prognosis for an individual is uncertain. Share it as a range and admit the uncertainty: "I worry that time may be shorter than we hope, possibly months rather than years." Never present a model's estimate as a fact about this patient.

## Hope and worry together
Pair hope with honest concern: "I hope the treatment keeps you well for a long time, and I also worry that you could become sicker. It would help to plan for both." This lets patients keep hope while still preparing.

## When the model is uncertain
A wide uncertainty range or disagreement between models means the data do not clearly place this patient. Let your own clinical assessment, the treating team's view and the patient's own sense of how they are doing lead. Uncertainty is still a good reason to ask about goals, because planning helps whatever happens.

## Respond to emotion before information
Strong emotions make it hard to take in facts. Pause, allow silence, and name what you see: "This is a lot to hear." Show understanding and offer support before giving more information.
